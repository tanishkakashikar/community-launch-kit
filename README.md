# B2B SaaS Community Launch Framework

Launching a vertical B2B SaaS community from zero. Sub-brand strategy, landing page built with Eng on Circle's developer platform, spaces architecture, founder-led programming, and a customer marketing engine that produces retention, case studies, podcast guests, and voice-of-customer input.

Most B2B SaaS communities die within 6 months because they launched without a clear purpose and got abandoned when engagement dropped. This is the framework I used to launch one that didn't.

**Stack:** Circle · Circle developer platform · HubSpot · SSO · Zoom · Notion

## Why I built this

Our buyer's peer network was where a lot of their decisions actually got made. We needed a space where the profession could talk to each other about the profession, with our brand as the host, not the topic. That framing turned the community into a retention and customer marketing channel instead of a customer support forum.

## What I owned

- Sub-brand strategy and positioning
- Platform selection and evaluation
- Sub-brand landing page built on Circle's developer platform in collaboration with Eng
- SSO integration scoping with Eng
- Spaces architecture built with CX
- Founder-led programming design
- Content repurposing loop from campaigns and podcast into the community
- Community-sourced customer marketing pipeline (case studies, podcast guests, VOC)

## How the system worked

**Sub-brand, not customer forum.** A customer forum is users asking product questions. A sub-brand community is professionals talking about the profession, with your brand as the host. The distinction changes engagement, tone, and business impact.

**Platform selection.** Evaluated Circle against Slack, Discord, and custom-built. Circle won on SSO integration, spaces architecture, content persistence, and native branding depth. Slack loses on content decay. Discord loses on professional context. Custom-built loses on time-to-value.

**Brand-native landing page.** Built the sub-brand's public landing page on Circle's developer platform in collaboration with Eng. The page functioned as the community's front door with SSO flowing through to the gated interior. Full brand consistency between the marketing landing page and the community itself, no visual drop between landing and joining. Doing this on the dev platform (rather than a standalone marketing site) meant one system, one login flow, one brand.

**Spaces architecture.** Top-level spaces designed with CX: public introduction, gated customer-only spaces, product requests open to all, founder office hours, general discussion, and events + resources. Each with a moderator and a posting cadence.

**Founder-led programming.** Monthly founder office hours. Direct founder posts multiple times a month. Weekly founder replies on product requests. Founder presence is the moat.

**Content repurposing loop.** Every campaign and every podcast episode gets extended into the community. Webinars become learning sessions. Surveys feed product roadmap discussions. Podcast episodes get community-first previews before public release. The community becomes the surface that makes every other channel work harder.

**Community as customer marketing engine.** The community is where case study candidates surface, where podcast guests get identified, and where voice-of-customer input is easiest to collect. Every moderator has a running list of members worth featuring.

## Tools used

Circle · Circle developer platform · HubSpot · SSO (built with Eng) · Zoom · Notion

## Results

- Sub-brand community launched end-to-end with a fully branded landing page and zero-friction SSO for customers
- Customer retention delta between community members and non-members was material enough to justify ongoing investment on retention alone
- Roughly 80% of case study candidates and podcast guests sourced from the community within 6 months

## Principles

- Sub-brand community outperforms customer forum for community-led growth
- The landing page and the community should live in the same system; splitting them breaks brand and adds a login step
- Founder-led is not optional; it is the moat
- Communities die when they become one-way broadcast channels
- Every campaign should have a community-facing repurpose
- Retention lift is the real ROI of community, not lead gen
- The community is the cheapest customer marketing engine you will ever build if you use it that way
